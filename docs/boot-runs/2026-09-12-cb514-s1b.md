## Findings

### Six clean boots constrain the failure rate, and not by much

Stated as arithmetic rather than as a feeling, because "it seems fine now" is how an intermittent
fault gets closed. With zero failures in six trials, the one-sided 95% upper bound on the per-boot
failure rate is **39%** — down from 63% after three, and still nowhere near a number that would
license the word *rare*.

Taking every boot anyone has recorded on this machine — the 2026-09-11 pair, one failure and one
pass, plus these six — the count is **1 failure in 8**. Point estimate 12.5%, 95% Clopper-Pearson
interval **0.3% to 53%**. An interval that spans two orders of magnitude is what one observation
buys, and the honest summary of the evidence is that the rate is unknown.

Trials are cheap and cumulative: 10 clean boots would put the ceiling at 26%, 30 at 10%. `S-1b`
records on every session boot from here, so this converges whether or not anybody is hunting it.

### The warm/cold hypothesis was tested and did not pay out

The 2026-09-11 report has an ordering that the first three boots here did not control for: the boot
that failed was the **first**, and the boot that found the camera came **after a power-cycle**. If a
cold start were the trigger — a warm reset leaves the embedded controller powered and the USB hub
further along, so the camera might land inside `build/init`'s `sleep 2` warm and race it cold —
then the cold boots were where the fault should have appeared.

They were clean. **That weakens the hypothesis and does not kill it**, and the arithmetic says by
how little: if cold boots failed half the time, three clean ones in a row would still happen 13% of
the time; at 30%, 34% of the time; at the 12.5% suggested by the overall count, 67% of the time.
Three trials cannot separate those.

What it does settle is that the next trial should not be more of the same. Cold has been tried.

### The one failure anybody has seen is uninterpretable in the vocabulary that now exists

Easy to miss and worth stating plainly: the 2026-09-11 boot that reported `No camera was found` ran
an image in which **all four `CameraReason` conditions printed that same sentence**. So it is not
known which of the four it was. `NO_CAPTURE_DEVICE` is the only one the deauthorisation story can
explain; `NO_USABLE_FORMAT`, `NO_BUFFERS` and `NO_FRAMES` all mean a video node existed and the
camera was opened, which no amount of `authorized_default=0` can cause.

That does not make the timing hypothesis wrong. It means the single observation the hypothesis was
built on cannot distinguish it from three other faults, and that the hypothesis has therefore never
had evidence that points at it specifically — only evidence compatible with it. The `sleep 2`
candidate remains the only candidate anybody has proposed, which is a statement about the supply of
candidates rather than about its merit.

The next occurrence will name its own condition. That is the whole of what #37 bought, and it is why
these six boots are worth recording even though none of them failed: the instrument is in place and
confirmed to be in the image before the fault next appears, rather than after.

### What a cold trial here can and cannot be

The Acer's internal battery keeps the embedded controller alive through a shutdown, so *unplugged
for five minutes* is an approximation of cold, not the real thing. A true cold start needs the
battery disconnected, which means opening the case. Written down so that these three clean boots are
not later cited as having ruled out a cold-start trigger — they did not test one.
